01

About the Book

Seven Minutes is a dark emotional novel about a boy named Marshall who hides his pain behind laughter. At first, his jokes seem harmless, but over time, they become a way for him to control, embarrass, and hurt other people.

The story begins when Marshall is trapped inside a storage room as smoke fills the air. With only seven minutes left, his mind begins to replay the memories that shaped him. Each memory reveals something important about his life: his childhood, his family, his school behavior, his bullying, his guilt, and the choices that led him to the locked door.

The novel explores how pain can change people when it is not understood or dealt with properly. It also shows that jokes, words, and small actions can affect others more deeply than we realize.

At its heart, Seven Minutes is a story about memory, regret, bullying, family trauma, and consequences. It reminds readers that every choice matters, and that the past does not disappear just because we refuse to face it.

Some memories don’t fade. They wait.

02

Character Profiles

Marshall

Marshall is the main character of Seven Minutes. He is not written as a simple villain, but as someone who becomes cruel because of pain, shame, and fear. As a child, Marshall used laughter to make his sister happy, but as he grew older, he started using jokes to hide his own hurt and make others feel small.

He often fools around in class, ignores responsibility, and acts like school and the future do not matter. Deep down, he is afraid of feeling weak or being judged. His final seven minutes force him to face the person he became.

Thomas

Thomas is one of the main victims of Marshall’s bullying. He is quiet, observant, and deeply affected by the way Marshall treats him. Over time, the pain builds until Thomas reaches a breaking point.

Thomas’s actions are not shown as right, but they show what can happen when someone is hurt for too long and nobody steps in properly. His character represents the damage bullying can cause when it is ignored.

Jenna

Jenna is Marshall’s younger sister. She represents innocence, childhood, and the softer part of Marshall’s past. In Marshall’s early memories, Jenna laughs at his jokes in a pure and loving way.

She reminds readers that Marshall was not always cruel. Her character helps show the difference between laughter that brings joy and laughter that hurts people.

Marshall’s Mother

Marshall’s mother is caring, but she often feels powerless in the family. She loves her children and wants peace, but she struggles to protect them from the damage caused by Marshall’s father.

She represents love, silence, and helplessness. Through her, the story shows how family problems can affect everyone in the home.

Marshall’s Father

Marshall’s father is strict, harsh, and abusive. His words and actions deeply affect Marshall’s confidence and emotional development. He makes Marshall feel ashamed, weak, and never good enough.

He plays an important role in showing how pain at home can shape how someone behaves outside the home. Marshall does not become cruel because of one moment, but because of years of shame and pressure.

Justin and Caleb

Justin and Caleb are Marshall’s friends at school. They often laugh at his jokes and encourage his bad behavior. Even when Marshall hurts others, their laughter makes him feel powerful.

They represent the role of bystanders and peer pressure. They show how people can support harmful behavior by laughing, staying silent, or treating cruelty like entertainment.

Ms. Carter

Ms. Carter is one of the teachers who tries to correct Marshall’s behavior. She notices that Marshall uses jokes to control the room and hurt others.

She represents guidance and warning. Her character shows that some adults try to help, but students must also be willing to listen and change.

Mrs. Allen

Mrs. Allen teaches the science lesson about the idea that memories can replay before death. This lesson connects directly to the title and structure of the book.

Her character helps introduce the concept of the final seven minutes and makes the story feel more meaningful.

03

Themes and Symbolism

Themes

Bullying

Bullying is one of the main themes of Seven Minutes. Marshall uses jokes to embarrass and control others. The story shows that bullying is not always physical; sometimes words, laughter, and humiliation can hurt just as deeply.

Memory

Memory is central to the book. Marshall’s final seven minutes are built around memories from his life. These memories force him to face the truth about who he was and how he became that person.

Regret

Marshall only begins to understand the damage he caused when it is too late. His regret is painful because he cannot undo his choices. This shows that understanding something late does not always fix the harm already done.

Family Trauma

Marshall’s home life affects his behavior. His father’s harshness, abuse, and pressure teach Marshall to hide his pain instead of dealing with it. The story shows how family trauma can shape a person’s emotions and actions.

Consequences

Every choice in the story leads somewhere. Marshall’s jokes, cruelty, school behavior, and refusal to change all build toward the final event. The book shows that actions have consequences, even when people try to ignore them.

School Behavior and the Future

The book also shows how fooling around in class, ignoring schoolwork, and pretending not to care can slowly affect a person’s future. Marshall acts like nothing matters, but his habits become part of a bigger pattern of avoiding responsibility.

Symbolism

The Yellow Toy Car

Represents Marshall’s childhood, innocence, and his bond with Jenna. It reminds readers that Marshall was once a happy child who used laughter in a harmless way.

The Storage Room

Represents consequences. Marshall is trapped physically, but also by his past choices.

Fire and Smoke

Symbolize guilt, danger, and the past catching up. Smoke makes it hard to breathe, just as guilt makes it hard to escape the truth.

Laughter

At first, joy and love; later, a weapon. Shows how something good can become harmful when used wrongly.

The Door

The locked door is the point of no return — some choices cannot be undone once they have gone too far.

Seven-Minute Timeline

Marshall’s final seven minutes are built around memories from his life. Follow the structure of the novel below.

  1. Chapter 1

    The Storage Room

    Marshall is trapped as smoke fills the air. At first he thinks it is a joke, but he soon realizes he may not survive. This chapter introduces the danger, the locked door, Thomas, and the memories about to begin.

  2. 1

    Minute One

    Marshall remembers being a young child with his yellow toy car and his sister Jenna — laughter that brought joy, not pain.

  3. 2

    Minute Two

    He discovers that jokes can make him feel powerful — making others laugh at someone else can protect him from feeling small.

  4. 3

    Minute Three

    Faith, family, and belonging — growing distance from his family and difficulty understanding what he believes.

  5. 4

    Minute Four

    Deeper family problems and pain caused by his father — lies, drinking, cheating, and emotional damage.

  6. 5

    Minute Five

    School, responsibility, and fooling around instead of thinking about the future — careless behavior becomes identity.

  7. 6

    Minute Six

    Marshall remembers becoming more openly cruel. His jokes and actions cause serious emotional harm, especially to Thomas.

  8. Chapter

    The Last Laugh

    One of the major moments that pushes Thomas too far — bullying becomes more than just jokes.

  9. Chapter

    The Door Stayed Closed

    Thomas and the consequences of revenge — his choice creates pain and guilt.

  10. 7

    Minute Seven

    Marshall finally understands the truth about himself, the pain he caused, and the choices that led him to the storage room.

  11. Epilogue

    After the Door

    What remains after Marshall’s death — the people left behind and consequences that continue.

Author’s Note

Seven Minutes is a story about choices, pain, and consequences.

I wrote this book to show that people are not always born cruel. Sometimes they learn to hide their pain in harmful ways. Marshall uses laughter to cover his fear and shame, but over time, that laughter becomes a way to hurt others.

I also wanted to show how fooling around in class, ignoring schoolwork, and acting like the future does not matter can slowly affect a person’s life. What seems funny or harmless at first can become a habit that leads to bigger problems.

The final seven minutes force Marshall to face the memories and choices he avoided. In the end, he realizes that words, jokes, and actions can affect people more deeply than we think.

This novel was written with the help of AI tools for brainstorming, structure, editing, and language refinement. However, the story idea, characters, themes, and final creative decisions are my own.

— Jason Bram

06

Behind the Cover Design

The cover of Seven Minutes was designed to match the emotional and psychological tone of the story. It is not meant to look like a simple horror book. Instead, it represents memory, regret, and the past returning at the final moment.

The boy’s dark silhouette represents Marshall and the hidden pain inside him. His face is turned down, which shows guilt, sadness, and reflection. Inside the silhouette is the storage room hallway, showing that the memory of the storage room has become part of him.

The storage room door represents the final consequence of Marshall’s choices. It is the place where he is forced to stop running from the truth. The glowing light behind the door suggests danger, memory, and the final moment before everything changes.

The yellow toy car represents Marshall’s childhood and his relationship with Jenna. It reminds readers that Marshall once had innocence, joy, and love before his pain changed how he treated people.

The cream, blue-grey, and dark colours were chosen to create a serious but emotional mood. The design is meant to feel like a memory fading onto paper.

“He laughed first. He understood last.” connects directly to Marshall’s journey. For most of his life, he laughed before thinking about the pain he caused. By the end, he finally understands — but only when it is too late.
